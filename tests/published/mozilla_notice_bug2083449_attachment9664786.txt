We are reaching out to you because you maintain a trust store for TLS certificates.

We became aware on the 17th of October that the private keys corresponding to 8 eMudhra Intermediate certificates have been compromised. These certificates are trusted by the Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple and Chrome root programs, as well as many of their downstream trust stores. 

6 of the intermediate certificates are for use in TLS, 2 are for code signing. They are listed at the end of this mail. 

Immediate action may be required to protect clients using your trust store or software package.

If your clients reliably check for current revocation information and will fail-closed (deny) if it is unavailable, you do not need to take action. The affected certificates were revoked by eMudhra on the 18th of October and Sectigo on the 19th of October. If your clients do not reliably check revocation information or fail open, you need to act.

If you have a mechanism for marking particular intermediates as distrusted, you should make use of it immediately and distribute the update to your users. Firefox and Chrome users have already been protected via their browser-specific blocking mechanisms (OneCRL, CRLSets).

If you do not have such a system, you should evaluate whether removing trust in the root certs which have signed the impacted intermediates is suitable. We are aware of three different chains to roots which may be in use:

    AAA Certificate Services (SPKI SHA-256: bd153ed7b0434f6886b17bce8bbe84ed340c7132d702a8f4fa318f756ecbd6f3)

    emSign Root TLS CA - G3 (SPKI SHA-256: c619e4780ed8e0abeda24ad732567de4383a7ea941338f6d1a7d1b49ac792fe9)

    emSign ECC Root CA - G3 (SPKI SHA-256: 8d417db2dd8bf5e3084d1e3f196d583849d81bdd4c00c70b9d39369e96b8c782)

Removing trust in all three roots (and any other roots in your trust store which have cross-signed the affected intermediates) will provide effective protection to clients. Although the impacted intermediates have not been used by eMudhra to sign customer certificates, there are other intermediates under these roots which have been used by eMudhra to sign customer certificates. Consequently, removing trust in these roots may cause some servers to become unavailable for your clients. 

We cannot provide advice on whether this decision is suitable for your clients, but we highlight that abuse of the compromised private keys is likely in the near future. 

To-date, certificate transparency logs do not show any issued certificates from the compromised intermediates, but this does not rule out the past creation of unlogged certificates which would be trusted by clients which do not enforce certificate transparency or creation in the future of logged certificates which would be trusted by clients who do not enforce revocation. 

At the time of writing, there is no evidence to suggest that other intermediates, or any roots operated by eMudhra or any other CA have been compromised.  

There is no embargo for acting on this information. The fact that the certificates were revoked is already public. However, we ask that you give trust store operators as much time as possible to act before the nature of the incident becomes more widely known and do not draw attention to this issue except for those who need to know in order to take action. 

This issue was first reported by Wayne, a member of the community. 

We believe transparency is essential to trust and will disclose further information about this incident as soon as is practical.
